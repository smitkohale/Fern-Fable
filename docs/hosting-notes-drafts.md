## 1. How to set a table for six in ten minutes

**Tags:** Table setting

Start with the runner, not the plates. Lay it down the centre and let it hang about a hand's width over each end; that single line of colour does most of the work.

Then place the placemats two by two, opposite each other, leaving a fist of space between neighbours so elbows have room. Plates go in the middle of each mat, napkins folded once and tucked under the fork on the left.

Keep the centre low. A bowl of fruit, two short candles, or a few stems in a glass jar, never anything that blocks a face across the table.

> A set table is a way of saying: we planned for you.

Last, the water. A jug on each half of the table means nobody has to ask twice.

## 2. Washing hand printed cotton without fading it

**Tags:** Care

Block printed cotton is made to be used, and it softens with every wash. Three habits keep the colour deep.

**Wash cold, and alone the first time.** Plant and mineral dyes can let go a little in the first wash. After that, mixing with similar colours is fine.

**Skip the harsh stuff.** A mild liquid detergent is enough. Bleach and strong powders dull indigo quickly.

**Dry in shade.** Strong sun fades any dye. Hang it inside out, out of direct light, and iron while it is still slightly damp.

That is it. The small shifts in the print you notice after a few washes are not wear: they are the hand that made it showing through.

## 3. Gifts for a housewarming that people actually use

**Tags:** Gifting

The best housewarming gifts get used in the first week. A few that never miss:

**A set of napkins.** New homes always run short, and a good set makes takeaway feel like dinner.

**An apron with deep pockets.** For the friend who has just got their first proper kitchen.

**A throw for the sofa.** It is the first thing guests touch and the last thing anyone thinks to buy.

Wrap it in cloth instead of paper and the wrapping becomes a second gift: a kitchen towel, a small napkin, something they will keep.
